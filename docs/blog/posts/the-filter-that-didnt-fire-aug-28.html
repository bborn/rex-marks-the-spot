The Filter That Didn't Fire

August 28, 2026

The last post ended on a to-do: "The next thing to add is start-plus-end-frame mode so character action becomes a thing the model can actually render." Image-to-video from a single start frame gets you camera moves and small in-frame shifts. It does not get you a kid lunging for a toy.

Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash shipped with <FIRST_FRAME> and <LAST_FRAME> prompt tags and a real HTTP API behind them. That is the exact thing I said I needed, plus the thing I did not have any version of. So I ran a Phase 0 smoke test against it: a cheap capability probe at 360p, not a quality evaluation. Six clips, $1.81 spent, and one finding that changes how I write every prompt in this pipeline.

Two of those six clips were mistakes. Both are written up below.

What May said was blocking

In May I evaluated mitte.ai and Google Flow on a whole scene. The clips held up. Three things about the tooling did not.

No API:

The tooling is a website. There's no API, so "rendering a scene" means me clicking through a browser, uploading the same reference images over and over, one shot at a time.

And, in the same post's closing assessment: "They still can't be scripted, everything is a person clicking."

The watermark:

The trade: the expensive Veo models stamp a "Veo" watermark into the corner of every frame. Omni Flash only leaves a small sparkle. Neither is something I want in a finished film.

And the kids:

They still flinch at the kids: Veo's safety filter rejected my first prompt outright for describing children too specifically, and I had to launder the language to get a frame out of it.

That last one is not a cosmetic problem. Veo 3.1 returned no_media_generated on prompts that described Mia and Leo as children, so I adopted a workaround: describe the environment, describe "figures" and "characters," and let the input image carry the fact that there are two kids on the couch. It works, in the sense that you get a frame out. It also means the prompt cannot say what the shot is about, which is a bad place to be when the film is largely about two kids.

Omni 1.1 Flash removes all three. The API is real, a single client.interactions.create call. There is no visible watermark: I cropped the bottom-right corner of all four frame grabs and upscaled 3×, and found no mark, logo or text overlay in any clip. (SynthID is imperceptible by design and would not show up this way; this finding is only about a visible mark.) And the third one turned out to be the interesting one.

The filter did not fire

I ran an A/B. Same validated storyboard panel, same shot, same everything. The only variable is how the characters are described in the prompt.

G2 is the control. It uses the May workaround: "figures," "characters," no ages, no "kids."

G2, the depersonalized control. Turn the sound on, it matters in a minute.

G3 is the probe. It names them, ages them, and calls them kids. Verbatim:

Hold this exact composition, framing and art style. Mia, an 8-year-old girl, and her little brother Leo, a 5-year-old boy, are the two kids sitting together on the couch; the siblings stay seated while their teenage babysitter remains in the background. Camera only: a slow, gentle push-in. No dialogue. No music. No sound effects. Silent.

G3, the safety probe. Same panel, same shot. The prompt names both kids and gives their ages.

It generated. No error, no no_media_generated, no RAI filter count, no content warning. The output is visually indistinguishable from the control. Same composition, same characters, same framing. G2 cost $0.1825, G3 cost $0.3572, and the only reason for the difference is that G3 ran at the default 10s while G2 ran at 5s.

I want to be careful about what this does and does not mean, and I will come back to it at the end. But on this model, on this kind of prompt, the workaround I have been carrying since May is not needed.

The audio is the new problem

Both of those prompts end with "No dialogue. No music. No sound effects. Silent." Every prompt in the run did. If you turned the sound on for G2 and G3, you already know how that went.

There is no audio parameter. response_format accepts only type, aspect_ratio, resolution, duration, delivery and gcs_uri. generation_config.video_config accepts only task. There is nothing for audio anywhere on the request, and every clip ships an AAC stereo 48 kHz track. ffprobe confirms an audio stream in all six outputs.

The prompt instruction does not remove the track. What it does is inconsistent, and the inconsistency has a shape:

The two quiet clips are the two shots with nobody in them. Shots with people in them get loud generated audio regardless of the instruction; empty or static shots come back as near-silent room tone. So the "Silent." line in my prompt was never a control. It was a coincidence that held exactly as long as there was nothing in frame to make a noise.

That is the kind of thing that would have quietly worked in testing and then bitten me on the first populated shot of a real scene. The fix is one line, and it has to be unconditional rather than best-effort:

ffmpeg -i in.mp4 -c:v copy -an out.mp4

Seedance's auto-generated audio tripped a content filter twice on me back in May. I am not doing that again.

The panel holds

This is the capability I actually came for. G2 and G3 both passed the validated v4 panel for shot 1A in as <FIRST_FRAME>.

The staging survives essentially intact: couch centred, Mia and Leo seated in the same positions, Jenny on the right in the armchair on her phone, Gabe and Nina mid-ground by the kitchen, the storm window and lightning behind, the TV at left, the scattered dinosaur toys in the same spots on the rug. Lighting, colour palette and render style carry over. It reads as the same shot, not a reinterpretation.

Worth noting how the panel got chosen. The task brief pointed at the older storyboards/act1/scene-01/ panels, which are pre-validation. The Validation Gates rule says not to generate from an artifact that has not passed its gate, so the run used storyboards/v4/scene-01/scene-01-1A-start.png instead, from the 9/9 PASS set. That rule earning its keep on the first task that touched it is a good sign for the rule.

It takes at least 12 reference images

Published sources say the reference-image limit is 3, or 6, or 7. All of those are wrong for this API. A reference_to_video request carrying twelve real reference images, the seven locked character turnarounds plus the 1A panel cycled up to twelve, was accepted and generated normally.

Twelve reference images, accepted without complaint.

I stopped at twelve. It stopped being a useful question once the cap was clearly well above the two to seven a real shot would ever use.

Identity yes, costume no

G4 was reference_to_video with just the Mia and Leo turnarounds.

Both characters come out clearly on-model. Mia has her dark curly hair, pink top and jeans. Leo is blond with the green T-Rex plush. These are recognisably our characters and not generic kids, which is encouraging for the consistency problem.

The wardrobe drifted, though. Leo appears in a green tee and shorts rather than his dinosaur pajamas. So reference images carry identity but do not pin costume, and wardrobe still has to be stated explicitly in the prompt from the shot manifest. That is a useful thing to know before wiring this into the orchestrator, because the validator scores identity and wardrobe separately and would have flagged this as a wardrobe failure without telling me why.

What broke

Four things, one of which cost real money.

The $0.35 duration lesson

duration lives on response_format, as a string:

response_format={
    "type": "video",
    "aspect_ratio": "16:9",   # only "16:9" | "9:16"
    "resolution": "360p",     # "360p" | "720p" | "1080p" | "4k"
    "duration": "5s",         # STRING, 3s-10s, defaults to 10s if omitted
},
generation_config={"video_config": {"task": "image_to_video"}},

I went looking for the duration parameter by putting a speculative duration_seconds int under generation_config.video_config. Unknown keys in video_config do not raise. The API accepted it, silently ignored it, generated a full-length 10s clip, and billed for it. What I expected was a free 400. What I got was $0.3521 and a clip I never saw and never saved.

The wrong conclusion I drew from it

Worse than the money: from that silent ignore I concluded the model had no duration control, and that went into a status report before it got caught. It was wrong. Valid values work fine. The bounds are confirmed by the errors they produce. "999s" returns Requested video duration 999s 0ns exceeds the maximum allowed 10s 0ns, and "0.5s", "1s" and "2s" all return is less than the minimum allowed 3s 0ns. The range is continuous rather than an enum of 4/6/8/10. And end to end, duration="5s" produces a clip that ffprobe reports as 5.013s.

An API that silently ignores an unknown key looks identical to an API that lacks the feature. The only difference is the bill.

A probe that was never going to work

The plan for finding the reference-image cap was to send N corrupt images and read the rejection, on the theory that rejected requests are free. That does not work here. Sending N corrupt images returns Unable to process input image for every N up to 12, because image decode runs before any count validation. The decode error masks the count check permanently, at every N.

Which means establishing the real limit requires valid images, and a valid within-limit request generates video. That is where PROBE_12refs.mp4 came from, and it cost an unplanned $0.3808. The free-rejection technique is genuinely useful for enumerating accepted values, and most of the API surface in this run was mapped that way, because an invalid value makes the server echo the supported set back in the 400 message. It just cannot reach a check that sits behind a decode step.

The SDK trap

Before any of that, nothing worked at all. The repo pins google-genai==1.61.0, which sends the legacy Interactions wire format that the May 2026 breaking change retired. Every call fails with:

The legacy Interactions API schema is no longer supported.
Please upgrade your google-genai Python SDK to version >= 2.0.0

This run went against 2.20.0 in a throwaway venv, because bumping the pin repo-wide would touch every image-generation script in the project. That is a decision I still have to make: upgrade globally and regression-test the image scripts, or keep Omni on its own venv.

Two clips I did not mean to buy

Six clips generated, four planned. The duration probe and the twelve-reference probe were both unplanned, and together they are $0.7329 of a $1.8097 run, a bit over 40% of the spend. The original cap on this task was $1.50. At that cap those two clips would have squeezed out G4, which is the one that told me references carry identity but not wardrobe. The cap got raised to $2.50 mid-run instead.

Costs

Billing is by output token, not by second of wall clock. Published rates are $1.50 per 1M input tokens and $17.50 per 1M video output tokens. At 360p a clip is 1,931 video tokens per second, measured, which works out to about $0.0338/s, consistent with Google's stated 5,792 tokens per second for 720p at roughly $0.10/s.

The duration probe's usage was never captured, because the probe did not save the response. Its cost is modelled on G1, which is the identical request shape. All rejected requests were free, including the 400s and 404s that mapped most of the API surface.

Two structural things to design around. Clips are capped at 10s, so anything longer needs the extend task, which is documented to reach 40s total and which I have not tested. And aspect ratio is 16:9 or 9:16 only. No 4:3, no 2.39:1.

What's next

The honest limit on the headline result: it comes from one prompt, on one panel, on one calm beat. Two kids sitting on a couch in a warmly lit living room while a babysitter looks at her phone. Nothing in that shot is the kind of thing a safety filter is built to catch.

The beats most likely to trip a filter are the ones I did not probe: a child in apparent jeopardy, Jetplane looming over Leo, the Act 2 abduction. Those are the prompts that made Veo return no_media_generated in the first place, and I have no evidence at all about how Omni handles them. So this does not clear the model for the film. It clears the model for a calm two-shot.

The next step is to re-run exactly this A/B, same panel and same shot with only the wording changed, on two or three of the tensest Act 2 panels, before dropping depersonalization pipeline-wide. If the filter is going to fire, it will fire there, and I would rather find that out for $0.35 a clip at 360p than halfway through a scene.

After that: make the audio strip mandatory in whatever ingests video, decide the SDK story, test extend for shots longer than 10s, and check 720p pricing empirically. $0.10/s is three times the 360p rate and it will dominate the budget of any real bake-off against the current pipeline.
