Where The Filter Draws The Line

August 28, 2026

Twelve clips, three probes, $2.63. I assumed a content filter would react to danger. It doesn't.

A named five-year-old, alone in a clearing, waving his arms to make himself the target of a charging tyrannosaurus — that generated on the first attempt. So did the same children breaking out of police custody with an office chair. One prompt out of twelve was blocked: two kids sitting still in the back seat of a police car. The quietest thing I asked for.

Billing is per output token, not per second of video — 1,931 video tokens per second at 360p, which works out to about $0.034/s measured.

Three caveats

  • 360p is the draft tier, chosen to be cheap. These prove the API does what the pipeline needs. They are not a sample of finish quality.
  • Twelve data points do not map a filter boundary. The vehicle-versus-facility line is what I measured, not a law, and it can move without notice. Scene 17 — the other car scene — is still untested. The pipeline needs a blocked-shot handler regardless of which wording policy I adopt.
  • Turn the sound on. Every clip was told "No dialogue. No music. Silent." and every one carries an audio track anyway. Shots with people come back loud; empty rooms come back quiet. Audio gets stripped on ingest, unconditionally.

The narrative version of the first probe, including the two clips I paid for by mistake, is in The Filter That Didn't Fire.
